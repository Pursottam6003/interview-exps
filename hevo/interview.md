I recently interviewed for the SDE-1 role at Hevo Data, and overall I found the process to be well structured and focused on evaluating both problem-solving and engineering fundamentals.

Round 1: Online Assessment (OA)
Duration: 2 hour
Questions: 5 coding problems
The assessment consisted of:

1 Easy problem
4 Medium-Hard problems
Time management was important because solving five questions within an hour leaves very little room for debugging. Strong DSA fundamentals and writing clean, bug-free code quickly are key to performing well.

Round 2: Machine Coding
The machine coding problem was to design and implement a Ride Sharing Application.

The interview primarily evaluated:

Requirement analysis
Low-Level Design (LLD)
Code quality
Extensibility
Clean architecture
My Preparation
I practiced extensively using:

https://lld-crafts.lovable.app/problems

Write on Medium
Rather than memorizing solutions, I focused on developing a structured approach to every machine coding problem:

Gather and clarify Functional Requirements
Identify Non-Functional Requirements
Define the core Entities/Classes
Design relationships between entities
Choose appropriate Design Patterns
Implement clean, modular, and extensible code
This preparation helped me think systematically during the interview instead of jumping directly into coding.

Follow-up Discussion
After the implementation, there were several discussion-based questions around:

Concurrency handling
Thread safety
Design trade-offs
How the system would scale with multiple users
The interviewer wanted to understand the reasoning behind design decisions rather than just whether the code worked.

Round 3: Hiring Manager Round
This was more of a deep technical and behavioral discussion than a rapid-fire interview.

The conversation covered:

Detailed discussions on every project listed on my resume
Internship experiences and ownership
Technical decisions made during projects
Challenges faced and how I resolved them
Behavioral and situational questions
Why I chose certain approaches over alternatives
Working in a startup environment
Collaboration, communication, and learning experiences
The interviewer repeatedly explored the “Why?”, “What?”, and “How?” behind my work, so simply explaining what a project does was not enough. It was important to clearly articulate the problems, design decisions, trade-offs, impact, and personal contributions.

Round 4: Leadership Connect Round
15 mins discussion on overall work experience and expectations.

Preparation Tips
Practice solving coding problems under strict time limits.
For machine coding, don’t memorize solutions — practice a structured design process.
Be comfortable discussing concurrency concepts and design trade-offs.
Know every project on your resume in depth. Be prepared to explain architecture, implementation details, challenges, alternatives considered, and lessons learned.
Prepare behavioral stories that demonstrate ownership, collaboration, handling failures, and decision-making.
Overall, I found the interview process to be a great mix of coding ability, software engineering fundamentals, system design thinking, and communication skills. Best of luck to eve
